## 1. The East African Community as a Commerce Region

The East African Community (EAC) comprises Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Somalia. For e-commerce purposes, the commercially active core is Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Rwanda. These four markets share mobile money infrastructure, overlapping logistics networks, and a growing cross-border trade corridor.

Kenya is the most developed digital commerce market in the region and is the primary target for Poetry in Motion's MVP. The regional context matters because creator supply, logistics partners, and platform expansion paths all operate across EAC borders.

---

## 2. Regional E-Commerce Characteristics

Cross-border trade within the EAC is constrained by inconsistent customs procedures, varying VAT treatment of digital goods, and fragmented logistics infrastructure. The AfCFTA digital trade protocol, if implemented, would reduce these barriers significantly. Until then, platforms operating across EAC borders must handle per-country payment rails, per-country tax compliance, and per-country logistics arrangements.

Mobile money penetration is high across Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Rwanda. M-Pesa operates in Kenya and Tanzania. MTN Mobile Money and Airtel Money cover Uganda and Rwanda. Cross-border mobile money transfers exist but carry higher fees than domestic transactions.

---

## 3. Regional Fashion and Creator Ecosystem

East Africa has a distinct and growing fashion identity. Nairobi, Kampala, and Kigali each have active designer communities. Nairobi is the regional hub for fashion weeks, design incubators, and export-oriented maker businesses.

Key characteristics of the regional creator ecosystem:

- Designers frequently sell across Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania through social media and informal networks.
- Physical retail presence in Nairobi often serves as the anchor for regional brand recognition.
- Fabric sourcing is partly local (Kenyan cotton, Ugandan bark cloth, Tanzanian kanga and kitenge) and partly imported from Asia.
- Production capacity is concentrated in Nairobi and Kampala. Small-batch manufacturing is the norm.

---

## 4. Regional Logistics

Last-mile logistics across the EAC is fragmented. The failure of Sendy in Kenya in 2023 is a direct warning against building logistics dependency on a single regional provider before product-market fit is established.

Viable logistics approaches for Poetry in Motion at MVP stage:

- Creator-managed delivery for Nairobi-based orders.
- Partnership with a single courier for Nairobi urban delivery.
- DHL for cross-border and export orders, following the ANKA model.
- Deferred: national and regional logistics network until volume justifies it.

---

## 5. Regulatory Environment

Each EAC country has its own data protection and consumer protection framework. Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 is the most developed in the region. Uganda and Tanzania have enacted or are developing equivalent legislation. Rwanda has a data protection law aligned with GDPR principles.

For Phase 1, Poetry in Motion's compliance obligations are Kenya-first. Regional expansion in Phase 3 or Phase 4 will require per-country legal review.

---

## 6. Platform Expansion Signals

ShopZetu has trialled expansion into Rwanda, Tanzania, and Uganda. ANKA operates across 47 African countries. Vivo Activewear has stores in Rwanda. These signals confirm that the EAC is a natural second market after Kenya for any platform that achieves product-market fit in Nairobi.

---

## 7. Implications for Poetry in Motion

| Priority | Implication |
|----------|-------------|
| P1 | Design the data model and payment abstraction layer to support multiple EAC currencies and payment providers from the start, even if only Kenya is live at MVP |
| P1 | Creator onboarding should not require a Kenyan bank account. Mobile money is sufficient. |
| P2 | Regional logistics RFQ: obtain quotes from at least three couriers covering Nairobi to Kampala, Nairobi to Dar es Salaam, and Nairobi to Kigali |
| P3 | Per-country legal review before expanding beyond Kenya |

---

## 8. Limitations

This document is based on desk research and inference from entity behaviour. No primary interviews with EAC logistics providers, customs officials, or cross-border sellers have been conducted in Phase 1. Phase 2 entity extensions should include at least one logistics RFQ and one interview with a designer who sells across EAC borders.
