## 1. Africa E-Commerce: Industry Overview

Africa's e-commerce market is structurally different from the global baseline documented in 01-global/INDUSTRY-ECOMMERCE.md. The differences are not deficits to be overcome. They are design constraints that determine what a viable platform must look like.

| Metric | Estimate | Source |
|--------|----------|--------|
| E-commerce users | 500 million or more (2025), approximately 40% penetration, approximately 17% user CAGR | US ITA via Trade.gov |
| Mobile share of web traffic | Approximately 69% mobile-only internet users in Africa (2021 baseline; trend continuing) | Trade.gov |
| Credit card penetration | Approximately 2% | Trade.gov |
| Debit card penetration | Approximately 10% | Trade.gov |
| Preferred payment methods | Cash on delivery and mobile money dominate | Trade.gov |
| Fashion online sales forecast | Approximately 13.4 billion USD fashion category (2025 forecast, regional) | Trade.gov |
| Clothing and footwear share of Africa e-commerce revenue | Approximately 24.4% of revenue (2024, segment leader) | Market Data Forecast |
| Africa e-commerce market size | Approximately 1.6 billion USD (2025) toward approximately 6.7 billion USD (2034) at approximately 17% CAGR | Market Data Forecast |
| AfCFTA digital trade potential | Approximately 113 billion USD e-commerce potential by 2029 if protocol is implemented | Algeria Tech, AfCFTA summary |
| Online fashion penetration | Approximately 4.2% of Africans shop fashion online versus approximately 24% in Asia | Omiren Styles |

The gap between 4.2% and 24% is not a problem. It is the market opportunity.

## 2. Structural Enablers

Mobile money, led by M-Pesa and its equivalents across the continent, has created a payment infrastructure that bypasses traditional card networks. Social commerce through WhatsApp, Instagram, and TikTok operates at a scale that formal e-commerce platforms have not matched. Improving 4G coverage and a young demographic profile sustain growth projections.

Source: BrandiQ Africa digital commerce 2026.

---

## 3. Structural Barriers

The UNCTAD eTrade for All framework identifies seven pillars of e-commerce readiness. Africa's weakest pillars are directly relevant to Poetry in Motion.

1. E-commerce strategy
2. ICT infrastructure
3. Payment solutions
4. Trade logistics and facilitation
5. Legal and regulatory frameworks covering data privacy and consumer rights
6. Skills
7. Access to finance

Sources: UNCTAD eTrade Readiness; Trade4MSMEs guide.

## 4. Africa Fashion Industry Context

Fashion is the leading category on major African platforms by revenue share. Rising global interest in African aesthetics exists, but the economic return is frequently captured by non-African brands unless platforms are designed to keep value local.

The informal sector is large. Instagram and WhatsApp selling, boutique sites, and physical market hubs coexist with formal platforms. Creator identity is strong; default personalisation is weak.

Source: Omiren Styles.

## 5. Pan-African Marketplace Landscape

### Jumia Group (NYSE: JMIA)

Founded 2012 in Lagos. First African e-commerce IPO. Operates in 8 countries. Fashion and beauty are among the top demand categories. The platform has expanded SME seller share from 40% to 60% of sellers (2025). Group GMV was 818.6 million USD in 2025, with Q4 GMV up 36% year on year. Profitability target is group breakeven in Q4 2026 and profitable FY 2027.

Classification: Infrastructure benchmark and logistics learning reference. Not a direct styling competitor.

### Kilimall International

Founded 2014 in Nairobi. Third-party marketplace, Kenya-focused with Uganda and Tanzania expansion. Estimated annual sales approximately 72 million USD (third-party estimate, not audited). Competes on price and variety in fashion SKUs.

Classification: Competitor on catalog breadth. Poetry in Motion competes on curation and creator relationship.

### ANKA (formerly Afrikrea)

Founded 2016, pan-African. Shop, Pay, and Ship infrastructure for Made of Africa products. Over 7,000 sellers from 47 countries. Total funding 13.5 million USD including a 5 million USD IFC and Proparco extension. DHL integration for global shipping.

Classification: Competitor on African maker platform positioning. Template for cross-border ship and pay infrastructure. Poetry in Motion differentiates on domestic Kenya styling and circular fashion first. ANKA is export rails.

## 6. Social Commerce as the True Incumbent

More than 80% of online shoppers in Africa use social media to discover and buy (reported, directional). Social commerce is estimated at five times the scale of formal e-commerce in Kenya by 2025 (reported, treat as directional). WhatsApp Business catalog plus M-Pesa person-to-person transfer is the default commerce stack for many buyers and sellers.

This is not a gap in the market. It is the market. Any platform that assumes users will bookmark a web app first will lose.

## 7. Implications for Poetry in Motion

| Priority | Implication |
|----------|-------------|
| P0 | M-Pesa, COD, and WhatsApp are industry defaults, not Phase 4 extras |
| P0 | Social-first go-to-market: WhatsApp order status, M-Pesa payment links, shareable quiz results |
| P1 | Creator tools must export collection drops to Instagram and TikTok assets |
| P1 | Do not out-spend Jumia on logistics nationwide at MVP |
| P2 | AfCFTA digital trade protocol is worth tracking for cross-border creator sales |
