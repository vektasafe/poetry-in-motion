## 1. What E-Commerce as an Industry Means

E-commerce is not one company type. It is a stack of industries.

| Layer | What it sells | Examples |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| Infrastructure | Hosting, payments, fraud, search | Stripe, Adyen, Cloudflare |
| Platforms | Store software and ecosystem | Shopify, Adobe Commerce, WooCommerce |
| Marketplaces | Liquidity, trust, discovery | Amazon, Alibaba, Jumia, Etsy |
| Merchants and brands | Goods and services | DTC brands, wholesalers |
| Logistics | Fulfillment, last mile, returns | DHL, Amazon FBA |
| Enablers | Ads, email, reviews, BNPL | Meta, Klarna |

Business definition: revenue at the platform level often equals take rate multiplied by GMV (gross merchandise value), not the full cart value. Investors weight net revenue and contribution margin over raw GMV (Equidam; CRV on GMV).

## 2. Global Market Size (2025 to 2026)

| Metric | Estimate | Source type |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Retail e-commerce as percentage of global retail | Approximately 20.5% (2025), toward approximately 22.5% by 2028 | Shopify enterprise blog |
| Global retail e-commerce sales | Approximately 6.9 to 7.4 trillion USD (2025 to 2026, narrow retail definition) | Analysis Atlas, Shopify |
| B2C e-commerce (broader reports) | Approximately 8.8 to 9.0 trillion USD (2026), toward approximately 17 to 19 trillion USD (2031) at 15 to 17% CAGR | Mordor Intelligence, Knowledge Sourcing |
| Cross-border e-commerce | Approximately 551 billion to 1.2 trillion USD (2025); faster growth than domestic | Analysis Atlas, Precedence |
| Largest country markets | China approximately 3.45 trillion USD, US approximately 1.6 trillion USD | EcomRankd 2026 |

Industry trend (2026): growth is decelerating but still positive in mature markets. Asia-Pacific and mobile-first emerging regions drive incremental share. Digital wallets account for approximately 53% of online transaction value globally (Mordor). AI personalisation and DSA/GDPR-class compliance are raising cross-border cost.

## 3. Business Models

### 3.1 Take Rates by Vertical

| Vertical | Typical take rate | Value provided |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| Commoditised goods | 5 to 10% | Aggregation, payments |
| Handmade and unique | 10 to 15% | Discovery, trust (Etsy, Depop) |
| General e-commerce | 12 to 18% | Fulfillment, buyer protection |
| Managed and services | 25 to 40% | Quality, disputes, vetting |

Source: Culta marketplace benchmarks 2026.

Poetry in Motion implication: creator share of 40 to 50% in product copy is inverted relative to platform take rates. If Poetry in Motion is the platform, a sustainable take rate for fashion marketplaces is typically 10 to 18% before logistics and support. Creator-first economics require a spreadsheet proof before public claims. See 05-critical-review/CRITICAL-REVIEW.md.

### 3.2 DTC Brand Unit Economics

Per-order profit after variable costs drives viability.

Revenue minus (COGS plus shipping plus payment fees plus packaging plus returns plus CAC amortisation) equals contribution margin.

A healthy marketplace has LTV:CAC greater than 3x on a contribution basis, with payback under 12 months (Equidam).

Source: Magnet Monster unit economics guide, updated 2025.

### 3.3 Platform Categories (2026)

| Category | Examples | Cost profile |
|----------|----------|--------------|
| SaaS storefront | Shopify, BigCommerce | Monthly fee plus payment percentage |
| Open-source self-hosted | Medusa, Saleor, Sylius, WooCommerce | Development and infrastructure; no license |
| Enterprise licensed | Adobe Commerce, Shopware Beyond | 22,000 to 200,000+ USD TCO per year at mid-market |
| Marketplace native | Amazon, Jumia | Commission plus ads |

Comparison framework: Qualimero platform comparison 2026.
