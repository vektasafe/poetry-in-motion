## 1. Fashion as an Industry

Fashion is a creative-manufacturing-retail system spanning three levels.

Upstream: fibre, mills, dyeing, CMT (cut-make-trim), logistics.
Midstream: brands, wholesale, licensing.
Downstream: retail, e-commerce, resale, rental.

Unlike generic e-commerce, fashion has high SKU complexity through size and colour matrices, high return rates, seasonality, and brand and marketing intensity.

| Metric | Estimate | Source |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Global fashion market (broad apparel) | Approximately 1.84 trillion USD revenue band | Latico, citing industry reports |
| Industry growth (2025 to 2030) | Approximately 2.8 to 4.1% CAGR, sluggish macro | Latico, McKinsey narrative |
| Fashion e-commerce share of retail | Approximately 48% of fashion retail online | Latico aggregator claim |
| Online fashion sales | Approximately 883 billion to 975 billion USD (2025 band) | Latico |
| Fast fashion market | Approximately 25 billion USD (2020) toward approximately 192 billion USD (2030) projected | Latico |

McKinsey State of Fashion 2025 (authoritative executive view):

- Revenue growth is low single digits in 2025. A cyclical slowdown has arrived.
- Non-luxury drives economic profit for the first time since 2010, excluding COVID years.
- Luxury is under pressure from tourism softness and China demand decline.
- E-commerce pure-play and mass online marketplaces face existential model stress: falling demand, rising CAC, and share price declines from pandemic highs.
- Winners are AI-powered curation, resale and off-price, and challenger sportswear.
- Consumers are price-sensitive, interested in dupes, experiencing climate anxiety, and affected by trade fragmentation.

Sources: McKinsey State of Fashion 2025; Fashion Dive summary; K3 Fashion Solutions recap.

## 2. Fashion E-Commerce as a Business

### 2.1 Channel Economics

| Model | Revenue logic | Margin pressure |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Owned e-commerce (DTC) | Full price minus COGS minus ads minus returns | Meta and TikTok CAC |
| Wholesale plus DTC | Wholesale certainty plus retail upside | Channel conflict |
| Marketplace seller | GMV minus platform fee minus ads | Take rate plus returns |
| Off-price and resale | Discount to MSRP; supply-driven | Sourcing inventory |
| Rental and subscription | Recurring; utilisation rate | Logistics and cleaning |
| Styling service | Subscription plus product margin | Inventory risk (Stitch Fix model) |

### 2.2 Return Rates and Fit

Fashion online return rates are often 20 to 40% or higher for apparel, varying by category and free-return policies. Zappos-style free returns buy trust but compress margin. The industry response has been better size data, AR try-on (still uneven), and narrower curated catalogs.

Poetry in Motion: quiz-based profiling addresses discovery, not garment measurements. Returns remain a risk until size and fit data exists.

### 2.3 Fast Fashion versus Ethical Positioning

| Player type | Business engine | Poetry in Motion contrast |
|-------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Shein and ultra-fast | Test micro-batches, reorder winners, social ads | Cannot compete on SKU velocity |
| Zara and H&M omnichannel | Supply chain speed plus stores | Competes on brand trust and stores |
| Luxury | Scarcity, heritage | Different customer segment |
| Resale | Supply acquisition plus authentication | Poetry in Motion circular module aligns here |
| Stitch Fix | Data plus inventory plus stylists | Closest analog to AI and quiz |

## 3. Resale and Circular Fashion

ThredUp 14th Annual Resale Report (GlobalData, released April 2026):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Global secondhand apparel (2025) | Approximately 257 billion USD, approximately 10% of apparel spend |
| Projected (2030) | Approximately 393 billion USD, approximately 9% CAGR |
| Growth versus new apparel (2025) | Secondhand approximately 13% versus new approximately flat; US secondhand approximately 19% |
| US market by 2030 | Approximately 78.8 billion USD |
| Primary constraint | Supply of secondhand goods, not demand |
| Discovery shift | AI and social feeds for resale discovery |

Sources: ThredUp IR release; Yahoo/WWD summary; Circular Resale analysis.

Industry meaning: circular fashion is economically real but entering a competitive maturity phase. Brands need operations including intake, grading, and pricing, not landing pages alone.

Poetry in Motion: trade-in and pre-owned UI without a reverse logistics partner is marketing only. See 05-critical-review/CRITICAL-REVIEW.md.

## 4. Technology and AI in Fashion (2025 to 2026)

McKinsey highlights:

- AI curation, content, and search are being deployed to combat choice overload.
- Generative AI is used for design and marketing, with efficiency gains and controversy around dupes.
- Inventory mismatch remains a core operations problem. AI does not replace ERP and planning alone.

Poetry in Motion prototype: mock AI with fixed confidence scores is misaligned with the industry move toward trustworthy AI discovery.

## 6. Weaknesses of This Desk Research

- Aggregator sites mix sources. Use McKinsey, ThredUp, and UNCTAD for claims.
- No primary interviews with Kenyan designers or retailers.
- Fashion 1.84 trillion USD versus apparel subsets differ in definition.
- ThredUp report is industry-sponsored. It is directionally useful, not neutral.

## 7. Recommendations for Poetry in Motion

| Number | Recommendation | Rationale |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| 1 | Pick resale or new-make wedge for MVP | Resale needs supply operations; new-make needs inventory |
| 2 | Curated catalog of 12 to 50 SKUs versus Shein scale | McKinsey: curation beats endless scroll |
| 3 | Measure returns from day one | Fashion unit economics live and die on return rate |
| 4 | Partner 5 to 10 creators with contracts | Creator economy needs legal framework and payouts |
| 5 | Defer verified sustainability until chain-of-custody exists | Regulatory and trust risk |
| 6 | Study Stitch Fix failures and successes | Personalisation is necessary but not sufficient |
